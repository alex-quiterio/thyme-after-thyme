# The Little Green Table

*a notebook of vegetable recipes*

## A note before cooking

This book began as a collection of handwritten recipes: measurements scribbled in notebooks, instructions written around crossed-out sentences, quantities remembered rather than measured, and the occasional mysterious "enough".

That is probably the right way to cook.

The recipes that follow keep that spirit, but make the dishes easier to follow. The originals were developed and clarified with care, and several new vegetable recipes joined them around the same philosophy.

The central idea is simple: **vegetables don't have to be a side dish.**

A good vegetable meal has structure. Something crunchy, something soft, something sharp, something rich, something fragrant. It can be humble and still feel abundant.

You don't need twenty ingredients. You need contrast.

Salt wakes things up. Acid gives them a backbone. Fat carries aroma. Heat creates sweetness and texture. Fresh herbs bring the whole thing to life.

Cook by taste, not by obedience.

## The five-part vegetable equation

When improvising, try to gather:

**1. a vegetable** — mushrooms, aubergine, cabbage, cauliflower, courgette, spinach, leek, peppers...

**2. a carbohydrate** — rice, potatoes, pasta, bread, noodles, puff pastry...

**3. something rich** — olive oil, butter, tahini, cheese, coconut milk, nuts...

**4. something sharp** — lemon, lime, vinegar, pickles, mustard...

**5. something aromatic** — garlic, ginger, herbs, spices, toasted seeds...

That's already a meal.
